# Post-Procedure Skincare: How to Care for Your Skin After a Facial, Peel, Microneedling, or Paramedical Tattoo

You had the treatment. Now you are home, standing in front of the bathroom shelf, and every bottle looks like a question. Is my cleanser too strong? Can I use my serum tonight? Post-procedure skincare is the part of the appointment nobody has time to explain in full, and it decides how comfortable the next week feels.

I formulated the products in this store for exactly this moment. In my clinical practice at [Healing Skin Medical Aesthetics](https://healing-skin.com/), I see freshly treated skin every day: glycolic facials, PRP and microneedling, paramedical tattooing over scars and stretch marks. This store is separate, the place where my formulating work lives, but both share the philosophy on my About page: "Balance first, barrier always, and never confuse aggression with efficacy." This guide turns that philosophy into a post-procedure skincare routine you can follow wherever you had your treatment.

## The First Rule of Post-Procedure Skincare: Your Provider's Instructions Win

Nothing I write here overrides the sheet your provider handed you. Different treatments, different depths, different skin. If they say keep the area dry for three days, keep it dry. If they say nothing on the skin for 24 hours, that includes my products.

Think of this article as the layer underneath their instructions: what to reach for once a cleanser, a moisturizer, or a sunscreen is allowed, and what to leave capped until they clear it. If anything here conflicts with what you were told, follow your provider, and call their office if unsure. That is not a disclaimer. It is the whole point of good post-procedure skincare.

One standing note from my About page: if you are in active chemotherapy or radiation, review any new topical, including everything below, with your oncology team first.

## What Freshly Treated Skin Needs (and What It Does Not)

Every professional treatment, whether it exfoliates, needles, or implants pigment, temporarily changes the surface of the skin. The barrier is working harder than usual, so the goal of post-procedure skincare is simple: give it what it needs and stay out of its way.

What it needs:

- **Barrier support.** Bland, comforting textures that sit on the skin without stinging.
- **Hydration.** Treated skin often feels tight and looks flaky. Water and lipids, pressed on gently, help it feel comfortable as it settles.
- **Sun protection.** Fresh skin and sunlight are a bad pairing. Once your provider allows sunscreen, use a mineral broad-spectrum formula every morning, plus shade and a hat. The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/) recommends broad-spectrum protection of SPF 30 or higher, reapplied outdoors, year-round.
- **Minimal ingredients.** Fewer products, fewer variables, so if something stings you know what caused it.

What post-procedure skincare does not need right now:

- Retinoids and retinol
- Exfoliating acids (glycolic, lactic, salicylic) and enzyme masks
- Vitamin C in strong concentrations
- Scrubs, brushes, and cleansing devices
- Fragrance and essential oils
- Anything new you have never patch tested

None of these are bad ingredients. Several are in my own line. They are simply the wrong tools for the first stretch of post-procedure skincare, and they go back on the shelf until your provider says otherwise. Everything in the [Calm Flow collection](/collections/calm-flow-collection) and the [sensitive skin care collection](/collections/sensitive-skin-care) was built to be the quiet alternative during that window.

## A Simple Three-Step Post-Procedure Routine

When your provider gives the go-ahead for products, this is the post-procedure skincare routine I give my own patients. Three steps, morning and evening, nothing extra.

**Step 1: Cleanse without rinsing.** [Calm Flow Micellar Water](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-calm-flow-micellar-water) is a no-rinse cleanser. Saturate a soft cotton pad, press it against the skin, and lift away. No tap water, no rubbing, no foaming agents that leave skin feeling stripped. It is my first-choice gentle cleanser after a procedure. If an area must stay completely dry, skip this step there.

**Step 2: Comfort and hydrate.** I formulated [Qi Restore Oil](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-qi-restore-oil), tagline "Nourishment for Healing Faces," to help treated skin feel soft and cushioned. Warm two or three drops between your fingertips and press, do not rub, onto damp skin. If you prefer a cream texture, [Calm Flow Water Cream](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-calm-flow-water-cream) does the same job in a lighter, water-based form.

**Step 3: Protect.** [Healing Support SPF 40 Sunscreen](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-healing-support-spf40-sunscreen) is a mineral, broad-spectrum SPF 40. Mineral filters sit on the surface of the skin, which is why I chose them for a mineral sunscreen after a procedure. Apply generously every morning once cleared, and reapply outdoors.

One addition: once an area is fully closed and healed, and your provider says silicone is appropriate, [Scar Repair Silicone Gel](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-scar-repair-silicone-gel) supports the look and feel of the settled scar. Never on open or scabbed skin. My [silicone scar gel guide](/blogs/news/silicone-scar-gel-guide) covers the how and why.

## Skincare After a Chemical Peel or Glycolic Facial

Skincare after a chemical peel is mostly about patience. The peel has done the exfoliating, and now the skin needs to shed on its own schedule. Expect tightness, some warmth, and flaking that can look uneven for several days. Do not pick, peel, or "help" a flake along.

Your routine: micellar water on a pad, a comforting layer of oil or cream, mineral sunscreen every morning. No acids, scrubs, retinol, or vitamin C until your provider clears them. If skin feels tight in the afternoon, a second light press of Qi Restore Oil is fine.

If you are having a series of glycolic facials, skin can be more sensitive as sessions stack. Keep your post-procedure skincare minimal for the whole series, not just the first week.

## Skincare After Microneedling or PRP

Skincare after microneedling asks for one thing above all: cleanliness. The treatment creates tiny channels in the skin, and for the first window your provider specifies, only what they applied or approved should touch it. Many providers send you home with a specific product for the first day or two. Use theirs, not mine, until they allow the switch.

When you are cleared, start with the three-step post-procedure skincare routine above. Micellar water on a pad is gentler than running water over sensitized skin. Qi Restore Oil should be pressed on, never massaged. Sunscreen is non-negotiable the moment you are allowed to wear it, because freshly treated skin is more vulnerable to the sun.

Hold off on active serums, fragranced makeup, sweaty workouts, and hot showers for as long as your provider tells you. Some redness is expected. Heat, swelling, or discomfort that worsens instead of settling is a call to your provider, not a skincare problem to solve at home.

## Skincare After Paramedical Tattooing (Scar, Stretch Mark, or Areola Work)

Paramedical tattooing is different from a facial because pigment has been implanted and the skin needs to close over it undisturbed. Aftercare is very specific, and I have laid out [the clinical day-by-day timeline](https://healing-skin.com/paramedical-tattoo-aftercare-timeline/) on the Healing Skin blog. Read it alongside your own aftercare sheet.

From the product side, the sequence is:

1. **Keep the area dry exactly as instructed.** No micellar water, no oil, no cream over the tattooed area during the dry phase. Post-procedure skincare for a tattooed scar begins with doing nothing to it.
2. **Silicone gel once the area is closed** and your provider says silicone is appropriate. Scar Repair Silicone Gel goes on as directed on the bottle. The clinic's protocol runs it twice daily for 4 to 6 weeks; your own provider's timing wins.
3. **Mineral SPF 40 daily** over the area once cleared. Sun exposure is the fastest way to change how the result looks.
4. **Retinol comes last.** My [Anti-Aging Body Moisturizer with Retinol](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-anti-aging-body-moisturizer-with-retinol) is a later-stage product for settled body skin, used on the treated area and the skin around it. The clinic's own guidance is after week 4, and only once your provider clears it.

Everything for this stage is grouped in the [Scar Care collection](/collections/scar-care).

## When to Reintroduce Your Actives

The end of strict post-procedure skincare is not a green light to put everything back at once. My rule in the clinic: one product at a time, a few days between additions, and a patch test on the inner arm first.

The order I recommend:

1. **Peptides first.** Peptides are typically the gentlest re-entry option, which is why [Qi Firm Peptide Renewal](/products/dr-rusnak-wellness-qifirm-peptide-renewal), my peptide serum for the look of scars and stretch marks, is tagged post-procedure in the store. If you want the background, read [what peptides are in skincare](/blogs/news/what-are-peptides-in-skincare).
2. **Hydrating and barrier products** you were using before, one at a time.
3. **Brightening and vitamin C products** next, once skin tolerates the above.
4. **AHAs, BHAs, and retinoids last**, starting at a lower frequency than before.

If anything stings or leaves skin looking redder the next day, stop, return to the three-step routine for a few days, and ask your provider. Post-procedure skincare done well is boring on purpose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does post-procedure skincare need to stay this minimal?**
It depends on the treatment and your provider's plan. A light facial may need days; a deeper peel, microneedling series, or paramedical tattoo may need weeks. Follow their timeline, not a generic one.

**Can I use my regular cleanser after a facial or peel?**
If it foams strongly, contains acids or fragrance, or requires scrubbing, set it aside until cleared. A no-rinse option like Calm Flow Micellar Water is the lowest-friction post-procedure skincare choice meanwhile.

**Is mineral sunscreen after a procedure better than chemical sunscreen?**
I prefer mineral filters on freshly treated skin because they tend to feel more comfortable. Chemical sunscreens are fine for many people once skin has settled. Either way, use broad-spectrum protection and reapply outdoors.

**When can I start silicone gel on a new scar or paramedical tattoo?**
Only once the area is fully closed and your provider says silicone is appropriate. Never on open or scabbed skin. See the silicone scar gel guide above.

**Can I use these aftercare products if I had my treatment somewhere else?**
Yes. Nothing here is specific to my clinic. The routine is for anyone with freshly treated skin, and your own provider's instructions still take priority.

## A Final Word

Post-procedure skincare is the least glamorous week in skincare and the one that matters most. Keep it simple, keep it covered from the sun, and let your provider's instructions lead.
